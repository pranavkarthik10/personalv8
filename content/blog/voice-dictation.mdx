---
title: "No, you don't need to pay for voice dictation"
publishedAt: "2026-01-26"
summary: "Open source/weight transcription models and apps get most of the way there."
---

Voice dictation has exploded recently, and for good reason. I think the inflection point for when this started was probably around when OpenAI released Whisper, when accuracy and speed shot up. 

It's definitely a good thing for a lot of people. You can ramble in any shape or form and get fast accurate transcription of whatever you said.

Unfortunately this has become a space that's been populated by VC-backed startups promising better voice experiences. Wispr Flow, Willow Voice, Aqua Voice, etc. They're all variations of the same thing: pay us monthly for slightly faster transcription and some polish. These companies have large-scale marketing/UGC campaigns to grab market share.

Then there's the indie side of the paywall. Monologue by Every, SuperWhisper, and more. Smaller teams, same model. High Subscription or one-time fees for conveniences.

Most of these startups and products offer a few things. Marginal latency improvements, packaged in a good interface, and quality-of-life features. Which are good if you *really* need them. 

Meanwhile, local models have gotten incredible. Whisper and Parakeet v3 run on almost any hardware and are really good. These offer a few pretty big benefits: no subscription, no sending your voice to someone's server, no privacy concerns. And many of these companies just run these or better STT models but are able to run inference much faster

Tools like Handy (handy.computer) wrap these models in the same sorta apps that the bigger companies do. They're also open source (https://github.com/cjpais/Handy), and are starting to offer a lot of the same features: custom dictionary, AI post-processing, etc. I've been running it as a daily driver for a while now with no real issues. 

Other alternatives exist too: [MacWhisper](https://goodsnooze.gumroad.com/l/macwhisper), [FluidVoice](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice), [HEX](https://hex.kitlangton.com/), also all open source. Now these aren't perfect yet, but get most of the way there for most people. So if you're wanting a local voice solution, it's worth trying one of these first.
